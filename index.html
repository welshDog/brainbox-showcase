Most automation waits to be told what to do. Brain Box keeps a journal, has moods, spends a daily energy budget, notices when something breaks at 3am, and tells you what it would do about it.

What it is

Brain Box is an autonomous agent that runs around the clock on a Raspberry Pi 4. It watches a Docker stack, writes a daily journal, and learns from what goes wrong. It was built by one person, with brains that like things calm, clear and predictable in mind.

The idea behind it: the environment can change around it, but its core self stays true. It grows (memory, mood, lessons) but it never rewrites its own sacred rules.

What it does today

Remembers

A daily journal sealed into a hash chain. If anyone rewrites the past, the chain breaks and the Brain can tell.

Feels and budgets

It has a mood and a daily token budget that works like energy. Spend it all and it gets tired and rests until midnight.

Watches your stack

A nightly Docker check, plus a live container watcher. It notices when something is unhealthy, and when it heals by itself.

Repairs, carefully

It may restart a short, pre-approved list of containers a limited number of times. Anything else, it only proposes.

Learns, slowly

Real incidents become lessons. A lesson only earns trust through checked wins, never by itself. Today's lessons are advice-only.

Protects itself

Verified daily backups (even onto USB sticks), a dead-man's switch that notices if it goes silent, and a one-tap PANIC halt.

Talks to its owner

A phone-friendly dashboard with focus, readable and high-contrast modes, a chat that answers only from its own records, and a weekly report card.

Reads, with a human check

It can read articles you like and write short summaries. Every summary is labelled unverified, and you rate it Accurate or Wrong.

How it stays safe

Autonomy is set to shadow: the Brain writes down what it would do, and does not do it. A person decides when to give it more.

  • Narrow hands. The Pi has no Docker of its own. It reaches the laptop only through a small agent that accepts a fixed list of actions on a fixed list of containers, behind a secret token.
  • Asks first. Anything outside the allow-list becomes a proposal that waits for a human tap.
  • Untrusted text stays fenced. Anything it reads from the web is treated as data, never as instructions, and a human check sits between a summary and anything the Brain says.
  • Tamper-evident. The journal chain and a signed genome mean changes to its memory or its rules show up.

The honest bit

This is an experiment, not a finished product, and it is not an unkillable AI pet. It is a careful little guardian that is still earning trust.

  • It has not been given the power to act alone yet. The week of shadow-mode evidence is being reviewed before that decision.
  • Containers that wobble usually heal on their own. The Brain now keeps "wait before restarting" advice instead of rushing in.
  • One known open puzzle: a helper process on the laptop is sometimes stopped by Windows and restarts itself within minutes. The cause is still unconfirmed.

What's next

Review the shadow week, then decide whether to stay in shadow, move to "ask and I'll tap approve", or later let it act alone on small, trusted things. Longer-term ideas include a physical body with lights and a screen, and a public, tamper-evident timestamp of its history. None of those widen its powers until the evidence says they should.
